# SuperOps

dotMARC can open a SuperOps ticket when an alert fires, resolve it when the alert resolves, and resolve the alert when
a tech resolves or closes the ticket in SuperOps. It can do this alongside HaloPSA, NinjaOne, ConnectWise and
Autotask: see [PSA integration](../psa-integration.mdx) for how several PSAs work together.

## 1. Create an API token

In SuperOps, open **Settings > My Profile > API Token** and generate a token. Copy it when it's shown.

Tickets are raised as the person who owns the token, so a dedicated technician account for dotMARC is the tidiest
choice. That account needs permission to view clients and to create and update tickets.

## 2. Find your subdomain and region

- Your **subdomain** is the part before `.superops.ai` in the address you sign in at. For `contoso.superops.ai` it's
  `contoso`.
- Your **region** is the data centre your account is hosted in: **United States** or **Europe**.

## 3. Connect dotMARC

In dotMARC, open **Manage > PSA settings** and choose the **SuperOps** tab.

1. Choose the **Region**, enter the **Subdomain** and paste the **API token**.
2. Select **Save SuperOps settings**.
3. dotMARC loads your SuperOps statuses, priorities, urgencies and categories as soon as the connection is saved.
   (**Load options from SuperOps** reloads them if you change them in SuperOps.) Choose:
   - the **New ticket status** (Open, unless you've renamed it)
   - the **Closed status** dotMARC moves a ticket to when its alert resolves (Resolved, unless you've renamed it)
   - optionally a **Priority** and **Urgency**
   - a **Category**. It's optional in dotMARC, but many SuperOps accounts won't resolve a ticket that has no category,
     so choose one if yours is among them.
4. Type the **Request type** alert tickets are created with. It's Incident unless you change it, and it must match a
   request type in your SuperOps exactly. SuperOps has no list of request types for dotMARC to load, so this one is
   typed.
5. Turn on **Enable SuperOps tickets** and save again.

## 4. Link clients

Tickets go to the SuperOps client linked to the domain's Group. On **Manage > Groups**, each Group has a
**SuperOps client** column. A Domain can override its Groups' client on **Manage > Domains**.

## 5. How resolving works

When an alert resolves, dotMARC adds a private note to the ticket saying so, then moves the ticket to your closed
status.

dotMARC checks its open SuperOps tickets every few minutes (every 5 by default, set with the
`Psa:PollIntervalMinutes` setting). A ticket a tech moves to your closed status, or to **Resolved** or **Closed**,
resolves its alert at the next check. If resolving a ticket fails, dotMARC keeps trying at each check. A ticket deleted
in SuperOps is no longer checked, and its alert stays open.

## 6. Test the integration

**Test the integration** on the SuperOps tab runs the whole lifecycle against your live SuperOps with the saved
settings. It creates a clearly marked test ticket, reads it back as open, resolves it, and reads it back as resolved,
which is exactly how dotMARC notices a tech's close. Real alerts aren't touched. If a step fails, it says what to check.

## Troubleshooting

- **SuperOps refused the API token.** Check the token is current, and that the subdomain and region match the address
  you sign in at.
- **Invalid status, request type, priority or category.** SuperOps matches these by name, so a value that doesn't exist
  in your account is refused. Reload the options and choose again, and check the request type's spelling.
- **A value is required for category.** Your SuperOps won't resolve a ticket without a category. Choose a **Category**
  on the SuperOps tab. dotMARC applies it to new tickets, and when resolving an older ticket that has none.
- **Tickets don't link to SuperOps.** dotMARC shows SuperOps ticket numbers without a link.
